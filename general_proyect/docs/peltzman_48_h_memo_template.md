# 48-Hour Translation Memo: Peltzman (1976)

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Core Question of the Text:** How do politicians distribute the costs and benefits of regulation across competing groups to maximize their total political survival, rather than serving just a single captured industry?

---

### 1. Core Causal Mechanism
* **Primary Actors:** The vote-maximizing politician, concentrated beneficiaries (e.g., domestic banks, bondholders), and diffuse cost-bearers (taxpayers, voters).
* **Objective Functions:** The politician seeks to maximize a political support function $M = M(\pi, P)$, balancing the political capital gained from delivering profits/returns ($\pi$) against the votes lost from higher prices or taxes ($P$).
* **Causal Chain:** Total regulatory capture is electorally suicidal $\to$ Politicians will not grant an industry pure monopoly rents if the resulting public backlash costs them the election $\to$ The system reaches an equilibrium where the marginal political gain from satisfying an interest group equals the marginal electoral loss from angering the public.

---

### 2. Standard Baseline Predictions
* **Cross-Subsidization:** The government never fully favors one side; it forces profitable sectors to subsidize politically sensitive ones (e.g., keeping transit fares low while allowing high utility rates for industry).
* **Shared Pain in Crises:** When a negative shock hits, the politician distributes the losses across *both* the concentrated industry and the diffuse public to minimize total vote attrition.

---

### 3. Argentine Sovereign Debt Translation
* **The Empirical Analogue:** The Executive balances the political support of the financial sector (avoiding default) against the electoral tolerance of the public (enduring fiscal austerity). 
* **Observed Policy Alignment:** According to the June 2026 OPC data, the Treasury faces a steep maturity wall for the rest of the year, projecting payments of \$120,235 billion in peso-denominated debt[cite: 1]. A massive concentration hits in December alone, with \$38,774 billion coming due[cite: 1]. Furthermore, 23.4% of the USD 246,515 million foreign currency debt is tied to the FMI[cite: 1]. To generate the fiscal surplus required to service these obligations without triggering a market crisis, the executive must impose domestic austerity. Peltzman’s model predicts exactly how the executive will distribute that fiscal pain without crossing the threshold of terminal voter backlash.
* **Points of Rupture:** Peltzman assumes a stable, continuous function of voter support. In Argentina, the trade-off is often non-linear: cutting spending might cost marginal votes, but failing to roll over debt could trigger a currency run and hyperinflation, which instantly destroys the government's entire political capital.
